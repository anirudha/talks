How it scores

Weighted by impact, not by count.

Borrowed from CVSS and SSL Labs: critical flaws cap the score, not just chip away at it. Fix the high-leverage rules first — that's where IS forces you to look.

40
Critical
Breaks the foundation. Missing service.name. Score gets capped here.
30
Important
Misleads analysis. Cardinality blow-ups, root CLIENT spans, k8s.pod.uid missing.
20
Normal
Hurts efficiency. Logs used as metrics. Excessive internal spans.
10
Low
Hygiene. SDK/runtime version drift, unsupported language versions.

Score = Σ (passed × weight) ÷ Σ (total × weight) × 100

What a single rule fix looks like

SPA-003 · Bound cardinality on span names.

Before · cardinality bomb
Each request = a new span name
name: "GET /users/8472/orders/9c1f"
name: "GET /users/2018/orders/3a7d"
name: "GET /users/9921/orders/be40"
name: "SELECT * FROM o WHERE id=8472"
Indexes blow up. Group-by is meaningless. Service-detail latency charts fragment into thousands of one-sample series.
After · routes & statements
Templated, low-cardinality
name: "GET /users/{id}/orders/{order_id}"
attrs:
  http.route: "/users/{id}/orders/{order_id}"
  user.id: "8472"
  db.statement: "SELECT * FROM o WHERE id = ?"
Aggregations work. Service maps are stable. AI agents can actually reason about /users/{id}/orders/{order_id} as a single endpoint.

How top-tier teams operationalize this

Weighted scoring → prioritized backlog.

The pattern at teams running OpenTelemetry at scale: stop trying to fix everything. Use the score to rank instrumentation debt the same way you rank security debt.

Step 1
Score every service, weekly
One number per service.name. Track delta over time. Make the score a tile on the platform team dashboard.
Step 2
Fix Critical → Important first
A single Critical fix moves the needle more than ten Lows. Backlog by impact tier — same prioritization muscle as CVSS.
Step 3
Make it a quality gate
CI fails when a service ships below threshold. Single Source of Truth becomes provable, not aspirational.
